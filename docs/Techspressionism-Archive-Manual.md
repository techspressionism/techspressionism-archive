# Techspressionism Archive: A Guide for Readers and Editors

*What the Techspressionism Video Archive is, how its transcripts are made, how accurate they are, and how you can help make them better.*

This guide is for anyone who reads the archive, cites it, or wants to help correct it. It does not cover how the archive is run behind the scenes.

**Status:** the archive is in beta. It is being tested by a small group before it is announced. Pages may change without notice, and the public "Suggest a correction" form described in section 5 is not switched on yet.

## 1. What the archive is

A searchable, citable transcript archive of the Techspressionism YouTube channel: the conversations, talks and salons of the Techspressionism art and technology community. Every transcript passage links to the exact second of the YouTube video where it was spoken, so a reader can always check the words against the recording.

| Type | Recordings | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Salons** | 103 | Regular online gatherings. Salons 2 and 3 were never recorded; recording began at Salon 8. |
| **Interviews** | 29 | One-to-one conversations. |
| **Roundtables** | 6 | Group discussions. |
| **Presentations** | 9 | The eight *Hello Uzbekistan* presentations and one talk recorded for the Pollock-Krasner House and Study Center (2021). |

About 218 hours of recordings in all. Each recording has one page with its transcript, and the whole archive can be searched at once. The archive's **About** page (linked at the foot of every page) explains what it holds, how the transcripts are made and how to cite a passage, and offers the recordings list and each transcript as plain text for download.

## 2. How to read a transcript page

- **Heading per speaker turn.** Each time the speaker changes, a heading shows the speaker's name (or **Unattributed**), a timestamp, and a link to watch that moment.
- **Watch links** open the YouTube video a few seconds *before* the passage, so you hear the start of the sentence. The timestamp shown is the exact time of the first word.
- **Every timestamp belongs to the YouTube video**, not to any other copy of the recording.
- **Links at the top** lead to the recording's page on techspressionism.com and to the video on YouTube.
- **Search** covers every recording. Each result links to the moment it was spoken.

## 3. How the transcripts are made

Transcripts are produced by computer and then improved step by step. In plain terms:

1. **Collect the facts** about each recording: title, date, speaker list, video length.
2. **Get the words.** Where Zoom produced a transcript, it is used because it labels who is speaking. Otherwise the audio is transcribed with **Whisper**, an open speech-recognition program. If neither is available, YouTube's captions are used.
3. **Match the video.** Some recordings were edited before they were uploaded (technical problems cut out, an intro added). Times are adjusted so that every timestamp matches the YouTube video, and speech that was cut from the video is removed.
4. **Find who is speaking.** Names come from Zoom's own labels, from the speaker list in the video description, or from a person who has listened and confirmed the name. If none of these is available, the passage is marked **Unattributed**.
5. **Fix known mistakes.** A list of known mishearings and the correct spelling of members' names is applied to every transcript.
6. **Human corrections** (section 5) are applied last, so they always win over the machine text.
7. **Build the pages** and the search index.

The process is repeatable: the same inputs always produce the same pages.

## 4. How accurate is it?

Honestly: **machine transcripts are never perfect**, and the archive does not claim they are. What has been checked:

| Question | Answer (September 2026) |
|---|---|
| Do the watch links land in the right place? | Yes. Timestamps were compared against YouTube's own captions: matching text lined up at the right times (75–93% agreement) and did not line up when shifted (1–4%). Zoom-based timings were checked the same way and corrected where they were off. |
| Are the names right? | A name is shown only when it is supported. Where two independent sources agreed, all 18 tested cases were right. Where a name could not be confirmed, the passage says **Unattributed**; the archive never guesses. |
| Are the words right? | Mostly, but errors remain, especially for names, art terms and technical vocabulary. A formal error rate has not been measured yet. |

The most common errors are misheard names and specialist words. That is where corrections help most.

## 5. How you can help correct it

**Suggest a correction** *(coming soon)*. Each paragraph will have a "Suggest a correction" link that opens a form on techspressionism.com with the recording, time and passage already filled in. You:

1. Play the moment with the watch link and listen.
2. Type the corrected passage.
3. Optionally add a note. Corrections are not credited on the pages (see section 8).

Every suggestion is read and approved by the archive's editor before it appears. Nothing you send changes the archive automatically.

**What helps most**
- Correct spellings of people, artworks, books, software and places.
- Words that were misheard so badly that the sentence makes no sense.
- Names that could be attached to a speaker you can identify with certainty (see section 7).

**What to avoid**
- Rewriting how someone speaks (section 6).
- Corrections you cannot check against the video.

## 6. Style rules for corrections

The archive is a **clean verbatim** transcript: it records what was said, not what could have been said.

1. **Keep the speaker's words.** Only "uh" and "um" are removed. "Like", "you know", repeated words, and unfinished sentences stay as spoken.
2. **Do not fix grammar** the speaker got "wrong", or tidy their phrasing. Correct only what the recording does not say.
3. **Spell names correctly.** Use the person's own spelling of their name, an artwork's title, a product's name.
4. **Titles of books** are italicized automatically; just spell them correctly.
5. **Punctuation** should help the reader follow the speech; it does not need to be perfect.
6. **One passage at a time.** A correction replaces one paragraph. Please do not merge or split paragraphs.
7. **Do not guess.** If you cannot tell what was said, leave it and say so in the note.
8. **Do not add** email addresses, phone numbers or private details, even if a speaker said them aloud.

---

## 7. Speaker names

Attribution is held to a strict standard because a wrong name is worse than no name.

| Label | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **A name** | The name was confirmed: Zoom labelled the speaker, or two independent sources agree, or a person who knows the speaker has confirmed it. |
| **Unattributed** | The speaker is not confirmed. The archive never prints a guess or a label like "Speaker 2". |

If you were there and can identify a speaker with certainty, say so in your note with the recording and the time. Please do not identify anyone you are not sure about.

---

## 8. Privacy and credit

- **No voiceprints and no face recognition are stored.** Voices are told apart only within a single recording, and never matched across recordings.
- **Email addresses are never stored** in the archive. Suggestion forms do not publish them.
- **Corrections are not credited on the pages.** A suggestion is treated as anonymous: a name or note given on the form is not stored in the archive.

---

## 9. Citing the archive

Each page shows a ready-made citation with the recording's title, date and the address of the page. When you quote a passage, cite the timestamp shown so that readers can go straight to the moment. Every paragraph of a transcript has a **Cite** button next to its timecode: it pauses the video and shows a citation for that paragraph (speaker, recording, date, timecode and a YouTube link that starts at that moment) in Chicago, MLA, APA, BibTeX or RIS, with **Copy Citation** and **Continue watching** buttons. Machine-readable citation details are in the repository's `CITATION.cff` file. The transcripts are licensed under Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 (CC BY 4.0).

## 10. Glossary

- **Beta**: the archive is being tested and is not yet announced.
- **Clean verbatim**: a transcript that keeps every word except "uh" and "um".
- **Recording**: one Salon, interview, roundtable or presentation.
- **Timestamp**: the time in the YouTube video at which a passage starts.
- **Timeline**: the clock a set of timestamps refers to. The archive uses the YouTube video's.
- **Whisper**: the open speech-recognition program that transcribes the audio.
- **Zoom transcript**: the transcript Zoom itself produced, labelled by speaker.
- **Attribution**: naming who is speaking.
- **Unattributed**: text whose speaker is not confirmed.
- **Correction**: a human fix to the machine text, reviewed by the editor.
