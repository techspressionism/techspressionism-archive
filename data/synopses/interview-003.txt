In Interview 3, artist Michael Pierre Price interviews Roz Dimon, who has worked with technology since the 1980s. She tells of [[1:57|growing up in Atlanta and driving to New York in 1981]], [[3:47|the shy child nicknamed for Leonardo who drew constantly]], and [[7:09|a painting of her mother and the women around her]]. She discusses [[11:55|the electric painting of Ed Paschke]] as an early influence and shows [[17:11|her pre-digital, multi-layered paintings]] of a city full of information. She explains [[20:29|her shift from oils to digital painting]], [[23:07|early gallery shows]], [[26:08|curating digital and computer art]] and [[28:10|the place of women in technology]]. Later she shares [[42:05|coded, animated work]], [[45:46|a permanent interactive installation]] and [[50:15|a community project with children during the pandemic]], before [[54:35|reflecting on what draws her to Techspressionism]] and where the movement may go.
