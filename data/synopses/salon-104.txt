Salon 104, "History, Theory, Connectivity" (March 5, 2026), is moderated by Renata Janiszewska and brings together artists and historians. Michael Pierre Price [[5:00|opens on the natural affinity between technology and Expressionism]], Helen A. Harrison [[7:43|notes that Techspressionism has become an international group]], and Catherine Mason [[10:24|places it alongside artists working in a precise manner]]. Colin Goldberg [[15:34|shows recent small paintings and his early influences]], and Harrison [[19:57|explains why Hans Namuth wanted to film Pollock]]. Mason describes early computer art, including [[23:28|work shown at the 1970 Computer 70 trade fair in London]], [[29:09|Lillian Schwartz's films]] and [[32:55|a 1973 Royal College of Art degree show piece by Anna Valentina Murch]]. Harrison discusses [[38:42|whether Pollock could have known about fractals]], and Mason recalls [[44:09|the 1958 Whitechapel exhibition of Pollock]]. Harrison talks about [[46:55|rethinking an artist's approach]], Stephen Carpenter [[53:15|shares his ties to Long Island]], Goldberg [[1:04:14|explains how he came up with the term in 2011]], and Annette Weintraub [[1:17:19|raises Joshua Reynolds and the manipulation of early photographs]].
