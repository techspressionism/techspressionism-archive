In Interview 30, Colin Goldberg talks with Brooklyn-based multidisciplinary artist Carla Gannis, whose work explores the "uncanny intersections" of physical and digital life. She traces her path from [[1:46|a small town in North Carolina]], where her father took her to the state art museum, through [[3:35|painting studies at UNC Greensboro and Boston]], and [[6:48|the 1990s decision to commit to digital media]]. She shares work that samples art history, including [[11:43|The Garden of Emoji Delights]], a mash-up of Bosch and emoji that became a giant print, an animation, 3D prints and AR, and [[16:42|a peepbox project]] built on Hoogstraten's illusions. She also discusses [[18:46|her early experiments with AI]], [[30:59|the difference between the GAN models of the 2010s and today's diffusion tools]], and [[32:17|her alter ego Jezebel Lanley]]. The talk turns to [[40:57|her summer residency at the Digital Stone Project in Italy]], where a robot and her own hands carved marble, and to [[51:33|new assemblage work made from obsolete headsets]] that considers e-waste, extraction and obsolescence. It closes with [[1:00:47|what Techspressionism means to her]].
