In Interview 24, Helen A. Harrison interviews Frank Gillette, a pioneering video artist. They talk about [[2:52|the medium and the message]] and [[10:42|the rhetoric of the advertising system]]. Gillette explains [[19:59|two phases of digital technology]], says he considers himself [[23:05|closer to the classical than the avant-garde]] and compares [[24:22|artists and their patrons from Giotto to Rubens]]. The conversation turns to [[29:08|critical theory and the Frankfurt School]], [[32:24|the loss of a sense of spirit]] and Harrison's question about [[33:13|Techspressionism as a concept]]. Gillette also describes [[39:36|a series of triptychs that hold up a mirror to the world]].
