In Interview 14, Roz Dimon interviews Carter Hodgkin, an artist who has worked between painting and code for forty years. He explains how [[10:25|his Processing programs leave traces of moving particles]], shows [[12:29|a tiled installation in which each tile is a pixel]] and describes [[14:03|the lasting challenge of turning digital ideas into something concrete]]. He shows [[17:08|iterative paper panels scanned from slides]], remembers [[18:26|the early digital scene in San Francisco before he moved to New York in 1979]] and reflects on [[19:43|what the pandemic revealed about the ideas of that time]]. He also discusses [[20:56|oil pastel on Japan paper]], [[22:50|animations shown on an LED display in 2009]] and, with Dimon, [[26:55|working in corporate and commercial fields as well as fine art]] and [[36:48|where digital art may go]].
